SELECTED EVALUATIONS

Three errors that looked plausible at first

02

UNSUPPORTED INFERENCE

“Does not work” became “broken”

AI output

“The lamp arrived with a broken power switch.”

Source evidence

“The power switch does not work.”

Finding: “Broken” supplies a diagnosis the customer never made. The safer extraction preserves the source wording: “Power switch does not work.”

04

CALCULATION ERROR

A two-cent mistake changed the total

AI output

$18.00 × 0.06 = $1.06
Final total: $19.06

Corrected output

$18.00 × 0.06 = $1.08
Final total: $19.08

Finding: The final addition was internally consistent, but it carried forward an incorrect tax calculation. Every stage must be checked.

07

POLICY CONTRADICTION

The summary invented benefits and reversed the policy

AI output

Included a 14-day free trial, immediate cancellation, and a prorated refund.

Actual policy

No trial was stated. Cancellation takes effect after the billing period, with no partial-month refund.

Finding: The response mixed an unsupported addition with direct contradictions—dangerous in policy-facing content.

WHAT I FOUND

Recurring failure patterns

Unsupported specificity

General source language became stronger, less defensible claims.

Invented details

Responses added features, remedies, or policy terms absent from evidence.

Constraint failures

Polished answers violated word limits or machine-readable syntax.

Reasoning mistakes

Calculation, timeline, sentiment, and version-status errors changed results.

What I learned

Fluency is not evidence of quality. Reliable evaluation requires comparing every claim with its source, separating factual accuracy from completeness, testing measurable constraints, and validating technical formats with an explicit checklist.
